Unit 4 · Weeks 12–13

Laser & Rack

Two weeks on the lasers. You learn how a laser actually cuts, measure the kerf it removes, then use that number to design a press-fit plywood rack for your fingerboards. Your teacher operates both lasers — you design and measure.

Week 12 Oct 26–30 · Kerf & settings card

How the Laser Works: Capabilities & Kerf

You learn what each laser can and can't do, then measure the kerf — the width of material the beam removes.

You will be able to

  • Explain how a laser cuts, scores, and engraves (vector vs. raster).
  • Choose the right machine for a material using the rule of thumb.
  • Predict, then measure, the kerf from a teacher-cut coupon (drawn − measured).
  • Record a kerf & settings card you'll reuse next week.

Kerf is tolerance for the laser: the beam always removes a little material, so a slot cut to the exact size comes out too big. You design around it. Note the two lasers differ — the LC40 cuts clear and colored acrylic; the Ador cuts dark/black acrylic only.

Card Kerf & settings card (20 pts)
Week 13 Nov 2–6 · Rack + fit log

Fingerboard Rack

You design a slot-together plywood rack that holds your fingerboards — using your Week 12 kerf so the joints press-fit with no glue.

You will be able to

  • Caliper the plywood thickness and an assembled fingerboard.
  • Apply slot width = plywood thickness − kerf for a glue-free press-fit.
  • Lay out a simple rack that fits the laser bed and the plywood sheet.
  • Press-fit it, load your boards, and log one change.

Tab-and-slot joints press-fit when each slot is cut narrower than the plywood by the measured kerf — a direct preview of the capstone's press-fit connections.
