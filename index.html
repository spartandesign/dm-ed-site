About this course

What students do

Digital Manufacturing & Engineering Design is an 18-week introduction to precise measurement, computer-aided design, and digital fabrication for middle schoolers. Students learn to read calipers, sketch and dimension parts, model in Onshape, and design for 3D printing and laser cutting — always pointed at one real object they can hold.

96 × 32 mmMETRIC DECK STANDARD

Every project points at the same object: a working fingerboard with real trucks and wheels. The deck is modeled to the 96 × 32 mm metric standard on purpose — it forces the inch→millimeter conversion (1 in = 25.4 mm) to become real, not abstract. Then twelve teams of three scale the idea up into one connected skate park for the capstone.

01 · Design in class

02 · Between sessions
The machine runs

The teacher runs the printer and lasers between sessions — students never operate a machine.

03 · Next session
Measure & iterate

Parts come back. Students caliper them, test the fit, log the result, and plan one change.

Grading

How grading works

Twelve graded deliverables across the first thirteen weeks total 255 points, and the five-week capstone is one project worth 100 points — 355 points in all. Week 3 is graded complete/incomplete. All work is submitted and graded in Canvas.

The lab

Four machines, one operator

The teacher operates every machine; students design and measure. Two lasers cover different jobs — knowing which one to reach for saves material and time.

3D Printer · FDM
Bambu Lab P1S
Bambu Studio · PLA · 0.2 mm
The course workhorse — every print, from the calibration cube to the capstone sections.
Laser · CO₂ 40 W
Dremel LC40
~20 × 12 in bed · manual focus
The heavy cutter: thicker and faster, and the only machine that cuts clear and colored acrylic.
Laser · Diode 20 W
Flux Ador
~16.9 × 11.8 in · autofocus · air assist
The finer-detail engraver. Cuts wood, plywood, leather, card, and dark/black acrylic only — no clear, no color printing.
CNC Router
X-Carve Pro 4×2
Easel
In the lab, but not used in this course — an optional demo only.

Which laser?

  • Clear or colored acrylic → LC40 only. The Ador's diode wavelength can't cut it.
  • Wood, plywood, leather, card, or dark material → either machine.
  • Thicker or faster cutting → LC40.
