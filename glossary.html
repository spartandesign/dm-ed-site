Glossary

The vocabulary that runs through the course — the words for measuring precisely, modeling in CAD, and making parts that actually fit. Each term notes the week it first shows up.

Wk 5
Tolerance
The allowed difference between the size you design and the size a machine actually makes. Managing tolerance is the spine of the whole course.
Wk 5
Interference
When two parts overlap and won't fit — too tight. The opposite of slop.
Wk 5
Slop
When a fit is too loose and wobbles. The opposite of interference; you aim to land between the two.
Wk 8
Clearance
Extra space added on purpose so parts fit: a hole = peg + clearance. How you avoid both interference and slop.
Wk 5
Deviation
Measured size minus designed size — how far off the machine was. You log it for the calibration cube.
Wk 1
Caliper
A precision tool that reads thickness, diameter, depth, and inside openings to a thousandth of an inch. It never leaves your side.
Wk 1
Dial vs. digital caliper
Two caliper types you'll read. The digital one toggles between inches and millimeters.
Wk 4
Parametric CAD
Computer-aided design where dimensions drive the model — change a number and the part updates. You use Onshape.
Wk 4
Onshape
The browser-based CAD tool for the course. You set your units to inches to match your calipers.
Wk 4
Sketch
A 2D outline on a plane that you turn into 3D with features like extrude and revolve.
Wk 4
Extrude
Pushing a 2D sketch into 3D to give it thickness.
Wk 6
Revolve
Spinning a sketch around an axis to make round shapes — how a ramp or bowl gets formed.
Wk 6
Mate
A relationship that joins parts in an assembly and controls how they move.
Wk 6
Degrees of freedom
The ways a part can still move after it's mated — the capstone teams use mates and DOF to connect sections.
Wk 4
Interference (CAD)
A check in CAD that finds where two bodies overlap before you ever print.
Wk 4
Measurement bridge
A notebook/CAD entry that links an on-screen dimension to a real caliper reading, keeping the model honest.
Wk 2
Orthographic views
The straight-on front, top, and side views of an object, drawn in correct projection.
Wk 2
Isometric view
A single 3D-looking view that shows three faces at once.
Wk 2
Multiview drawing
A drawing that combines the orthographic views plus dimensions and a title block.
Wk 2
Title block
The labeled corner of a drawing: name, object, date, and units.
Wk 11
Working drawing
A complete, dimensioned drawing a manufacturer could build the part from — generated from your model.
Wk 5
FDM
Fused Deposition Modeling — the 3D-printing method the Bambu P1S uses, laying down melted plastic layer by layer.
Wk 5
PLA
The plastic filament used for printing — the house default at a 0.2 mm layer height.
Wk 5
Slicer
Software (Bambu Studio) that turns a 3D model into machine instructions and estimates print time.
Wk 8
Design for printing
Adjusting a model so it prints reliably — managing overhangs, thin walls, and tiny features.
Wk 12
Vector vs. raster
A laser cuts/scores along vector lines and engraves images as a raster (row-by-row) pattern.
Wk 3
Score
A shallow laser line that lets material fold without being cut through — how the display stand bends.
Wk 12
Kerf
The width of material the laser beam burns away. A slot cut to the exact size comes out too big — so you design around it.
Wk 13
Press-fit (tab-and-slot)
A joint that holds with no glue because the slot is cut narrower than the material by the measured kerf.
Wk 14
Connection standard
The shared peg size, spacing, and clearance every capstone team builds to, so 12 sections snap together.
Wk 14
Capstone
The final project: twelve teams build one connected skate park, graded as a single 100-point project.
Wk 5
Single-operator loop
The course rhythm: you design in class, the teacher runs the machine between sessions, you measure and iterate next time.
