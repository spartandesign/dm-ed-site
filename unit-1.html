Unit 1 · Weeks 1–3

Foundations:
Measure & Sketch

Before anything gets printed or cut, you learn to measure precisely and draw clearly. These three weeks build the two habits the whole course depends on — calipers in your hand and dimensions on your page — and end with your first laser-cut object.

Week 1 Aug 13–14 · Measurement log

Measurement & Calipers

You learn to read dial and digital calipers to a thousandth of an inch — the skill the whole course rests on.

You will be able to

  • Read both a dial and a digital caliper to 0.001 in.
  • Use the right caliper jaws for a thickness, an outside diameter, a depth, and an inside opening.
  • Zero the tool, square the jaws, and measure with light, consistent pressure.
  • Switch a digital caliper between inches and millimeters.

A part is only as good as your ability to measure it. From now on the calipers never go back in the drawer — you'll check every print and every laser cut for the rest of the semester.

Log Completed measurement log + two reflection sentences (10 pts)
Week 2 Aug 17–21 · Dimensioned drawing

Sketching & Dimensioning

You turn a real object into a dimensioned multiview drawing — front, top, and side — plus an isometric view.

You will be able to

  • Draw the three standard views (front, top, side) in correct projection.
  • Add an isometric view to show the object in 3D.
  • Dimension each feature once, using extension and dimension lines.
  • Add a title block: your name, the object, the date, and the units.

This is the shared language of engineering drawings — dimension once, clearly, so anyone can build the part from your page.

Drawing Dimensioned multiview + isometric drawing (10 pts)
Week 3 Aug 24–28 · Build + fit check

Fingerboard Display Stand

Your first laser-cut object: a chipboard stand that holds a fingerboard, cut from one shared Onshape master file.

You will be able to

  • Read a flat (2D) layout and picture how scored folds become a 3D object.
  • Tell cut lines apart from score lines on a laser file.
  • Assemble the stand and check that the fingerboard actually fits.
  • Explain why a fold needs a score line, not a cut.

Your teacher runs the laser — you design and fit. This is the first make → measure → fit loop, and a preview of the press-fit joints you'll cut in Unit 4.

Build Assembled stand + fit check (graded as completion)
