Unit 3 · Weeks 8–11

The Fingerboard

Now the hero object gets real. You design the 96 × 32 mm deck for printing, print it and fit real trucks in millimeters, customize and ride-test it, then produce a proper working drawing.

Week 8 Sep 28–Oct 2 · DFP checklist

Design for Printing

You prepare your deck model to actually print well — and learn the conversion that makes the metric deck possible.

You will be able to

  • Apply 1 in = 25.4 mm to move between inch and millimeter dimensions.
  • Apply the clearance rule: a hole = peg + clearance, so parts fit.
  • Spot and fix print problems — overhangs, thin walls, tiny features.
  • Build a design-for-printing checklist for your deck.

Too tight = interference, too loose = slop. Clearance is how you land in between — the same idea you'll use for truck fit, kerf, and the capstone.

Checklist Design-for-printing checklist (20 pts)
Week 9 Oct 5–9 · Working fingerboard

Print the Deck + Fit the Trucks

Your teacher prints your 96 × 32 mm deck. You mount real trucks and wheels — measuring in millimeters — and log the fit.

You will be able to

  • Switch your digital caliper to millimeters to measure real hardware.
  • Check that the printed truck-holes match the trucks, and fix them if not.
  • Assemble a working fingerboard with trucks and wheels.
  • Log the fit: what was tight, what was loose, what you'd change.

This is the metric standard paying off: the 96 × 32 mm deck meets real trucks, and the inch→mm conversion from Week 8 becomes load-bearing.

Build Working fingerboard + fit log (25 pts)
Week 10 Oct 12–16 · Customized deck

Now Shred! — Customize & Ride-Test

You customize your deck — graphics, shape, concave — your teacher reprints it, and you ride-test the result.

You will be able to

  • Modify your deck model for grip, shape, or personal graphics.
  • Predict how a change will affect the ride before printing.
  • Ride-test the customized deck and compare to your prediction.
  • Log what worked and what you'd change next.

Iteration in action: change one thing, test it, keep what works. That loop is the heart of engineering design.

Build Customized deck + ride-test log (20 pts)
Week 11 Oct 19–23 · Working drawing

Working Drawing of the Deck

You produce a complete, dimensioned working drawing of your deck — the kind a manufacturer could build from.

You will be able to

  • Generate orthographic views straight from your Onshape model.
  • Dimension every feature once, clearly, with sensible tolerances.
  • Add a title block and any notes a builder would need.
  • Read your own drawing back to check nothing's missing.

This closes the loop from Week 2's hand drawing — same language, now generated from a real 3D model.
