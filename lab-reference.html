Lab Reference

The teacher operates every machine; students design and measure but never run a printer or a laser. This page is the quick reference for the lab — the machines, which one to reach for, the measurement standards, and the standing safety rules.

Which laser?

  • Clear or colored acrylic → LC40 only. The Ador's diode wavelength can't cut it.
  • Wood, plywood, leather, card, or dark material → either machine.
  • Thicker or faster cutting → LC40.
Measurement

How we measure

Inches are the default. The course measures in inches with calipers in every unit; the dial and digital calipers from Week 1 never go back in the drawer.

Millimeters where the part is metric. The fingerboard deck and its holes (Week 9), the working drawing (Week 11), the laser kerf and rack (Weeks 12–13), and the capstone connection standard are all in mm.

1 in = 25.4 mm is taught in Week 8 and used for real in Week 9, when the 96 × 32 mm deck meets real trucks. The digital caliper's in/mm toggle, introduced in Week 1, becomes load-bearing there.

The refrain, all semester: too tight = interference, too loose = slop. Clearance, kerf, and the capstone connection standard are all ways of landing in between.

Safety

The standing rules

3D printer

  • The nozzle (~200 °C) and bed are hot during and just after a print — students never reach into the machine.
  • The teacher removes parts with the proper tool; the build area stays ventilated.
  • No student touches the printer mid-print. The single-operator rule is the safety rule.

Lasers

  • The teacher operates both lasers; students never run them.
  • Fume extraction runs the whole time and the lid stays closed while cutting.
  • Approved materials only — never PVC, vinyl, or unknown plastics; they release toxic gas.
  • Laser + wood or paper is a fire risk: never leave a job unattended, keep the bed clear, keep the extinguisher accessible.
